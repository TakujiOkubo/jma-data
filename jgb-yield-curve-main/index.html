JGB yield curve model — Main Forecast

Model vintage: August 2026, origin July 2026. Estimates are revised periodically, and updated estimates are available for paid users upon request.

The JMA yield curve model splits each JGB yield into interest-rate expectations and a term premium, letting Japan’s long-run interest rate move with its fundamentals. This page carries the full decomposition — history and projection — under our Main scenario for Bank of Japan policy. A fuller explanation of the model sits at the bottom of the page, and the argument behind it is in our report, The Long Climb in JGB Yields Is Nearly Over. The charts below are interactive. Hover for exact values, zoom, and download the data behind each chart.

What this projection assumes

Bank of Japan policy. Two 25 basis point increases, in October 2026 and March 2027, taking the policy rate from 1.00 per cent to a terminal 1.50 per cent. This is an input, not a model output: everything projected below is conditioned on it.

The US term premium. A rise in the US 10-year term premium following the change of Federal Reserve chair at the end of May 2026, peaking at 122 basis points at end-November 2026 with half the rise retained thereafter. About a quarter of a US term-premium move passes into superlong JGB yields. The shaded band on the charts is a one-standard-deviation range around this assumption alone.

For more detail on these assumptions and the reasoning behind them, please see our forthcoming report.

About the model

In our reading, most of the historic rise in JGB yields between 2022 and 2026 came from a higher expected path of policy rates and a normalization of term premia as the BoJ withdrew its unconventional easing. Term premia at the longer end have largely finished normalizing, and our forecast sees the Long Climb in JGB yields nearly over. The blocks below explain the model behind that reading.

What the model is. Our model is a variant of Adrian, Crump and Moench (2013), the standard framework for splitting a bond yield into a risk-neutral component and a term premium. We make one Japan-specific modification. A standard model assumes the economy’s long-run interest rate is stable. Following Bauer and Rudebusch (2020), we let the underlying stars move over time, anchoring the long-run rate to the equilibrium real rate plus trend inflation expectations, each measured for Japan from published sources. Term premia are benchmarked against their 2004 to 2012 averages, the last era of free JGB pricing.

Why we do not use a standard model. A model with a fixed long-run rate has no place to record Japan’s post-2022 rise in inflation expectations except in the term premium. It therefore reads a genuine repricing of expected rates as a surge in risk compensation. We have seen estimates of a surging JGB term premium circulating in the market. In our judgement they mostly come from standard models and significantly overestimate the premium. As of mid-July 2026, a standard model puts the 10-year term premium around 1.2%. Our estimate reads 0.6% to 0.7%. The chart below shows the difference.

What goes in. Spot yield estimates are our own, based on daily JGB pricing information published by the Japan Securities Dealers Association. For more details on our spot yield curve estimate, please see here. Our equilibrium real rate is a weighted average of the natural-rate estimates compiled by the Bank of Japan (see reference below). Trend inflation expectations also come from the BoJ’s Composite Index of Inflation Expectations (see reference below).

What we publish, and what we do not. Details of the yield curve model are proprietary, but I am happy to discuss if your interest is of an academic nature. This page carries monthly estimates of spot yields, risk-neutral rates and term premia at 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 40 years, from 2002, together with a three-year forecast of the spot curve.

References

Adrian, Crump & Moench (2013), “Pricing the Term Structure with Linear Regressions,” Journal of Financial Economics, 2013, vol. 110, issue 1, 110-138. This is the base model. The New York Fed publishes daily ACM term-premium estimates for US Treasuries at its “Treasury Term Premia” data page.

Bauer & Rudebusch (2020), “Interest Rates Under Falling Stars,” American Economic Review vol. 110, issue 5, 1316-54. It is a seminal paper demonstrating the legitimacy of time-varying r* and π* as determinants of the yield curve.

Bank of Japan Monetary Affairs Department, “Developments in the Natural Rate of Interest and the Assessment of the Degree of Monetary Accommodation”. BoJ Review 2026-E-4 — the six natural-rate estimates our r* draws on.

Osada & Nakazawa (BoJ, 2024), “Assessing Measures of Inflation Expectations: A Term Structure and Forecasting Power Perspective”, BoJ Review 2024-E-4. The Composite Index of Inflation Expectations (BoJ-CIE), the basis of our π*, was published in 2024. It has not been officially updated, although we have seen BoJ officials use an updated image chart.
