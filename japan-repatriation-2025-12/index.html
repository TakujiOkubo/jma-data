From 2014 to 2025 Japan's investors bought more foreign securities than they sold, ¥13.6 trillion a year on average, about 2.3% of GDP, and that steady outflow was one of the forces behind the weak yen. In 2026 the flow turned. Banks are selling foreign bonds and buying JGBs; households keep buying foreign equity through funds, but pension funds sell almost as much. The result is a small net sale.

This page tracks that turn in the weekly and monthly data, in the order of the report, and is refreshed as the data arrive. The argument is in JMA's report Yen Repatriation: The Tide Is Turning. Positive numbers are net purchases of foreign securities (money leaving Japan); negative numbers are net sales. These statistics record purchases and sales; they do not show whether the proceeds are converted into yen.

Foreign bonds

Banks, the government's reserves, pensions and insurers own Japan's foreign bonds. Banks are selling while pension trust accounts keep buying. City banks hold most of the banks' foreign securities and have cut them in 2026. At home, regional banks buy medium-term JGBs and city banks 10-year JGBs.
