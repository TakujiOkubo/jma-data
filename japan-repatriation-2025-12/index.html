Free edition: data through December 2025, frozen at that date and not refreshed. The paid edition carries every month since, including the 2026 turn, and the data behind each chart.

For fifteen years Japan's investors bought more foreign securities than they sold, ¥11.6 trillion a year on average in 2011–2025, and that steady outflow was one of the forces behind the weak yen. This free edition shows the record up to December 2025: who bought, who sold, and who holds Japan's money abroad.

What happened next, in 2026, is the subject of JMA's report Yen Repatriation: The Tide Is Turning and of the paid edition of this page, which is refreshed as the data arrive. Positive numbers are net purchases of foreign securities (money leaving Japan); negative numbers are net sales. These statistics record purchases and sales; they do not show whether the proceeds are converted into yen.

The record to 2025

Japan bought foreign securities almost every year from 2011 to 2025. Who owns the foreign equity and who owns the foreign bonds decides where a turn would show first.

Who buys foreign bonds, and who buys foreign equity

Banks, the government's reserves, pensions and insurers own Japan's foreign bonds. Pension trust accounts bought foreign bonds in every year from 2011 to 2025; banks bought or sold with the cycle. In foreign equity, households keep buying through investment trusts, pension funds sell against them, and GPIF's weights stay close to its 25% targets.
